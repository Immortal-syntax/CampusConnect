## 1. Examination Administration & Attendance Rules

### 1.1 Final Examination Timetable
- **Scheduling Authority:** Final examination dates, times, and room assignments are designated by the Office of the Registrar according to the master class meeting matrix.
- **Instructors Prohibited From Rescheduling:** Course instructors are strictly prohibited from holding final assessments during the final week of regular instruction ("Dead Week") or changing the published final exam timetable without prior written approval from the Provost.
- **Mandatory Attendance:** Students must report to their assigned examination room at least 10 minutes prior to the scheduled exam commencement with a physical student identification card.

### 1.2 Examination Conflicts & Rescheduling Petitions
A student is eligible for an official examination schedule adjustment under the following verified conditions:
1. **Three Exams in 24 Hours:** A student with three or more scheduled final examinations occurring within a continuous 24-hour window may petition to move the middle examination to an alternate designated day.
2. **Direct Time Conflict:** Two examinations scheduled at the exact same hour due to cross-listed or asynchronous courses.
3. **Documented Medical or Personal Crisis:** Severe acute illness or family bereavement requiring immediate medical documentation submitted to the Dean of Students within 48 hours.

**Filing Deadline:** Conflict rescheduling petitions must be submitted to the respective academic department no later than 14 instructional days prior to the start of the final examination period.

## 2. Quality Point Grading System & GPA Calculation

### 2.1 Grade Points Scale
Grade points are assigned to letter grades based on a four-point (4.00) quality standard:

| Letter Grade | Quality Points (Per Credit Unit) | Qualitative Description |
| :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **A** | 4.00 | Outstanding Achievement |
| **A-** | 3.70 | Excellent Competency |
| **B+** | 3.30 | Very Good Competency |
| **B** | 3.00 | Good Competency |
| **B-** | 2.70 | Above Average Competency |
| **C+** | 2.30 | Average Competency |
| **C** | 2.00 | Satisfactory (Minimum Passing for Major Courses) |
| **C-** | 1.70 | Sub-standard, Passing |
| **D+** | 1.30 | Deficient Passing |
| **D** | 1.00 | Barely Passing (Electives Only) |
| **F** | 0.00 | Failure (Zero Credit Units Earned) |

### 2.2 GPA Calculation Formula
Grade Point Average (GPA) is computed by dividing the total number of quality points earned by the total number of graded credit units attempted:

$$\text{Term GPA} = \frac{\sum (\text{Course Credit Hours} \times \text{Grade Quality Points})}{\sum \text{Graded Credit Hours Attempted}}$$

- **Non-Graded Marks:** Grades of **W** (Withdrawn), **P** (Pass), **NP** (No Pass), and **AU** (Audit) carry no quality points and are excluded entirely from both the numerator and denominator of the GPA formula.
- **Cumulative GPA:** Represents the aggregate calculation across all undergraduate semesters completed at the institution.

## 3. Formal Grade Appeal Procedure

Students who believe a final course grade was assigned based on arbitrary, capricious, or discriminatory evaluation may initiate a formal grade appeal. Grade appeals must follow a mandatory three-stage sequence:

### Stage 1: Informal Instructor Consultation
- Within 15 business days following the posting of final term grades, the student must confer directly with the course instructor to review grading criteria, rubric evaluations, and mathematical calculations.
- If the instructor identifies a clerical error, a *Change of Grade Form* is routed directly to the Registrar.

### Stage 2: Formal Departmental Review
- If an agreement is not reached in Stage 1, the student may submit a written grievance to the Department Chair within 10 business days of the instructor's decision.
- The written appeal must include: the course syllabus, assignment prompts, graded coursework, and evidence demonstrating arbitrary deviation from syllabus grading rubrics.
- The Chair reviews evidence with both parties and renders a written recommendation within 10 business days.

### Stage 3: College Academic Appeals Board
- If the student disputes the Department Chair's findings, an appeal may be escalated to the College Dean.
- The Dean convenes an Academic Standards Hearing Board comprising two faculty members and one student representative.
- The Hearing Board's ruling represents the final institutional determination.

## 4. Academic Integrity & Honor Code

### 4.1 Prohibited Conduct
1. **Plagiarism:** Presenting the ideas, expressions, code, data, or written phrases of another person (or generative AI systems without explicit instructor authorization) as one's own work.
2. **Unauthorized Collaboration:** Sharing homework, programming files, or examination solutions with peers when collaborative work has not been authorized in the syllabus.
3. **Cheating & Fabrication:** Using unapproved notes, mobile devices, or external websites during assessments; falsifying scientific data or historical citations.
4. **Multiple Submissions:** Submitting substantially the same academic work for credit across two different courses without prior permission from both instructors.

### 4.2 Sanctions & Disciplinary Records
- **First Infraction:** Discretion of the instructor, ranging from a grade of zero on the specific assignment to an automatic **F** in the course, accompanied by an official reprimand on file with the Office of Academic Integrity.
- **Repeat Infractions:** Referral to the University Honor Council, resulting in mandatory academic suspension (one to two semesters) or permanent expulsion with transcript notation.
